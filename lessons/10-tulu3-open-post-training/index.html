Introduction: Fully Open Post-Training

In Lessons 02, 04, 06, and 08, you learned about individual post-training techniques: RLHF, DPO, GRPO, and Constitutional AI. But how do these pieces fit together into a complete, production-ready post-training pipeline? And how do you know if your trained model actually works?

The Tülu 3 Vision

Lambert et al. (2024) from Allen Institute for AI introduced Tülu 3, a family of fully-open state-of-the-art post-trained models alongside all the data, code, training recipes, and evaluation framework needed to reproduce them.

Key insight: Open post-training means more than releasing model weights. It means releasing the complete recipe—curated training data targeting core skills, on-policy preference data, training infrastructure, rigorous evaluation with development/unseen splits, and comprehensive decontamination—so the community can reproduce, adapt, and improve upon the work.

What Makes Tülu 3 Different?

Tülu 3 is the first fully-open post-training recipe that achieves state-of-the-art results:

  • Complete openness: All training data, code, evaluation suite, and decontamination tools released with permissive licenses
  • Multi-stage pipeline: SFT → DPO → RLVR (a novel RL method with verifiable rewards)
  • Rigorous evaluation: Development and unseen evaluation splits to detect overfitting to benchmarks
  • Aggressive decontamination: Systematic removal of evaluation data leakage from training sets
  • State-of-the-art results: Tülu 3 70B surpasses Llama 3.1 Instruct, Qwen 2.5, GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4o mini, and matches Claude 3.5 Haiku

What You'll Learn

  • The end-to-end Tülu 3 pipeline: data curation → SFT → DPO → RLVR
  • How to curate multi-skill training data (synthetic + real-world prompts)
  • On-policy preference data generation for DPO
  • RLVR: reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (vs learned reward models)
  • Development vs unseen evaluation splits and why they matter
  • Decontamination: detecting and removing benchmark leakage
  • Results: how each stage (SFT → DPO → RLVR) improves core skills

The Tülu 3 Pipeline: Four Stages

Tülu 3 follows a four-stage post-training recipe, starting from pretrained base models (Llama 3.1 8B, 70B, 405B):

Stage 1: Data Curation

Curate prompts from public datasets (with verified licenses and clear provenance) and synthetically generate skill-specific prompts. Decontaminate against the evaluation suite to prevent leakage. Result: 23.3M prompts covering general chat, math, coding, safety, instruction following, and multilingual tasks.

Stage 2: Supervised Finetuning (SFT)

Finetune the base model on 939,344 prompt-completion pairs selected from the curated prompts. Mix general-purpose data (WildChat, OpenAssistant) with skill-specific synthetic data (math, coding, instruction following). Result: Tülu 3 SFT checkpoint.

Stage 3: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

Generate on-policy preference data by sampling completions from Tülu 3 SFT and comparing them to outputs from other models. Apply length-normalized DPO on preference pairs. Result: Tülu 3 DPO checkpoint with improved chat and instruction-following.

Stage 4: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

Apply RL to tasks with verifiable ground-truth outcomes (math, instruction following). RLVR gives a constant reward only when the completion is verified correct—no learned reward model needed. Result: Tülu 3 final checkpoint, pushing math and IF performance even higher.

Why This Pipeline?

Each stage targets different aspects of model behavior:

  • SFT: Teaches core skills (math, coding, safety, knowledge recall) and establishes the foundation for instruction-following.
  • DPO: Refines output quality, reduces harmful/unhelpful responses, and improves conversational coherence by learning from preference comparisons.
  • RLVR: Pushes performance on verifiable tasks (GSM8K, MATH, IFEval) where we can objectively check correctness—no subjective reward model needed.

Key Design Choices

  • Development/unseen splits: Tülu 3 Eval includes both splits to detect overfitting to specific benchmarks during training
  • Decontamination: All training data is systematically checked for overlap with evaluation benchmarks and contaminated examples are removed
  • On-policy preference data: Generated from Tülu 3 SFT's own outputs, reflecting its actual behavior distribution (not off-policy data from different models)
  • Verifiable rewards over learned RM: For math and IF, RLVR uses deterministic verification instead of a learned reward model, avoiding reward hacking

Synthetic Data Generation with Personas

To fill gaps in public data, Tülu 3 generates synthetic prompts using persona-driven synthesis (from Chan et al., 2024). The key idea: condition on diverse personas (e.g., "A machine learning researcher focused on neural networks") to steer the LLM toward generating varied, skill-specific prompts.

Example: To generate math prompts, sample a persona like "An actuary calculating insurance premiums" and prompt GPT-4 to create a relevant math problem from that persona's perspective. Repeat with ~250K different personas to get diverse coverage.

Why personas?: Without personas, LLMs fall into repetitive patterns (mode collapse). Personas provide natural diversity by anchoring generation to different viewpoints and expertise areas.

Data Provenance and Licenses

Tülu 3 carefully tracks the provenance of all datasets and only includes those with clear, permissive licenses. Notably, ShareGPT was excluded due to questionable legal provenance (shared by users without consent for training use). All Tülu 3 datasets have verified licenses.

Decontamination: Removing Evaluation Leakage

A critical but often-overlooked step: checking training data for overlap with evaluation benchmarks. Tülu 3 developed a decontamination toolkit that:

  • Compares training prompts against all evaluation sets (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, IFEval, etc.)
  • Removes exact matches and near-duplicates (using text similarity)
  • Reports contamination statistics for transparency

Stage 2: Supervised Finetuning (SFT)

The SFT stage finetunes Llama 3.1 base models on 939,344 carefully selected prompt-completion pairs. The goal: establish strong foundational capabilities across all core skills while maintaining balance.

Data Mixture: General + Skill-Specific

The SFT mixture balances two types of data:

General-Purpose Data (~40%)

Real-world chat prompts (WildChat), volunteer-annotated conversations (OpenAssistant), expert-curated tasks (No Robots), and classical NLP tasks (FLAN v2). These provide diversity and general instruction-following capability.

Why important: Models trained only on skill-specific data become narrow specialists; general data maintains broad competence and conversational coherence.

Skill-Specific Data (~60%)

Synthetic math prompts (Persona MATH, Persona GSM, OpenMathInstruct), coding prompts (Persona Python, Evol CodeAlpaca), precise instruction following (Persona IF), safety (CoCoNot, WildJailbreak), and multilingual (Aya subset).

Why important: General data alone doesn't suffice for challenging tasks like MATH or precise instruction following. Targeted data pushes performance on specific capabilities.

Iterative Mix Refinement

The SFT mixture wasn't chosen arbitrarily—it was refined through multiple rounds of experimentation guided by the development evaluation suite. The process:

  1. Establish upper bounds: Train skill-specialized models (e.g., math-only, code-only) to see maximum achievable performance on each skill
  2. Mix and measure: Train models on different data mixtures, evaluate on development benchmarks
  3. Identify gaps: If math performance lags behind the math-specialist, increase math data proportion and re-train
  4. Check for interference: Ensure adding more of one skill doesn't degrade others (e.g., adding too much code data might hurt conversational quality)
  5. Converge: Iterate until the general model approaches specialist performance on each skill without sacrificing others

Key Findings from SFT Experiments

  • Real-world prompts matter for chat: WildChat (real user queries) is crucial for AlpacaEval performance; synthetic data alone produces stilted conversations
  • Targeted data scales capabilities: Adding Persona MATH prompts improved MATH score by 20+ points without hurting other skills
  • Safety data prevents regressions: Including WildJailbreak and WildGuardMix substantially improved safety scores with minimal impact on helpfulness
  • Decontamination is non-trivial: Several public datasets had significant overlap with evaluation sets, requiring careful filtering

SFT Training Details

  • Base models: Llama 3.1 8B, 70B, 405B
  • Training data: 939,344 prompt-completion pairs
  • Training procedure: Standard next-token prediction (causal language modeling)
  • Result: Tülu 3 SFT checkpoint—strong foundational instruction-following with balanced capabilities

Stage 3: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

After SFT establishes foundational skills, DPO refines the model by training on preference comparisons: pairs of responses where one is preferred over the other. Tülu 3 uses on-policy preference data generated from the SFT model itself.

On-Policy Preference Data Generation

Traditional preference datasets (e.g., UltraFeedback) are off-policy: they contain responses from models other than the one being trained. Tülu 3 generates on-policy preferences by:

  1. Sample prompts: Select prompts from the curated dataset (same sources as SFT)
  2. Generate completions: For each prompt, sample multiple completions from Tülu 3 SFT and from other models (e.g., Llama 3.1 Instruct, GPT-4)
  3. Pairwise comparison: Use a strong model (e.g., GPT-4) or human annotators to judge which completion is better
  4. Create preference pairs: Store (prompt, chosen_completion, rejected_completion) tuples

Why on-policy? On-policy data reflects the SFT model's actual output distribution. If you train DPO on off-policy data (from a different model), the preference model learns about mistakes the current model doesn't make and fails to address its actual failure modes. On-policy data targets the model's specific weaknesses.

Length-Normalized DPO

Tülu 3 uses length-normalized DPO (a variant of standard DPO from Lesson 04). The key modification: normalize the DPO loss by completion length to prevent the model from gaming the objective by generating very short or very long responses.

Standard DPO (Lesson 04)

Directly optimizes the implicit reward function encoded in the preference data. Can be gamed by length: the model might learn that shorter responses are "safer" (fewer opportunities to make mistakes) and collapse to terse outputs.

Length-Normalized DPO

Divides the log-probability of each completion by its length before computing the DPO loss. This prevents length exploitation and encourages the model to produce appropriately detailed responses.

DPO Training Details

  • Starting checkpoint: Tülu 3 SFT
  • Preference data: 271,409 preference pairs (8B model) and 334,302 pairs (70B model) from Table 15; 425,145 prompts selected for preference stage per Table 7
  • Data composition: Mix of on-policy Tülu 3 SFT preferences and off-policy UltraFeedback (decontaminated)
  • Algorithm: Length-normalized DPO
  • Result: Tülu 3 DPO checkpoint with improved chat quality and instruction-following

DPO Stage Improvements

From Table 5 (70B model, development benchmarks):

  • AlpacaEval 2: 26.3% (SFT) → 49.6% (DPO)—massive chat quality improvement
  • IFEval: 82.1% (SFT) → 82.6% (DPO)—modest instruction-following gain
  • Safety: 94.4% (SFT) → 89.0% (DPO)—slight regression (recovered in RLVR)

DPO's main contribution is improving conversational quality (AlpacaEval) while maintaining technical capabilities. The safety regression is expected and addressed in the final RLVR stage.

Stage 4: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

The final stage introduces RLVR, a novel RL-based method for tasks with verifiable ground-truth outcomes. Unlike RLHF (which uses a learned reward model) or RLAIF (which uses AI-generated preference labels), RLVR uses deterministic verification.

What is RLVR?

RLVR applies reinforcement learning to tasks where you can objectively check if the answer is correct:

  • Math (GSM8K, MATH): Parse the model's final answer and compare to the ground truth
  • Instruction Following (IFEval): Check if the response satisfies all specified constraints (e.g., "include exactly 3 bullet points," "start with the word 'Hello'")
  • Code (HumanEval, in principle): Run unit tests against generated code

RLVR Reward Function

For each task, define a verifier function that checks correctness. The reward is:

reward = 1.0 if verifier(completion) == correct, else 0.0

That's it. No learned reward model, no preference labels, no AI judge. Just pass/fail verification.

How RLVR Differs from RLHF and RLAIF

RLHF (Lesson 02)

Reward model: Trained on human preferences
Pros: Works for subjective tasks (helpfulness, harmlessness)
Cons: Learned RM can be exploited (reward hacking), expensive human labels

RLAIF (Lesson 08, Constitutional AI)

Reward model: Trained on AI-generated preferences
Pros: Cheaper than RLHF, scalable
Cons: Still susceptible to reward hacking, AI judge can be wrong

RLVR (Tülu 3)

Reward: Deterministic verification (correct/incorrect)
Pros: No reward hacking (ground truth is objective), simple and reliable
Cons: Only works for tasks with verifiable outcomes

GRPO (Lesson 06)

Reward: Verifiable (unit tests for code/math)
Relation to RLVR: GRPO is a specific RL algorithm; RLVR is the general paradigm of using verifiable rewards. Tülu 3 uses PPO-style RL with RLVR rewards.

RLVR Training Pipeline

  1. Select verifiable tasks: Tülu 3 uses GSM8K, MATH, and IFEval
  2. Create training prompts: Sample prompts from each task's training set
  3. Generate completions: Use the DPO checkpoint to sample multiple completions per prompt (via VLLM for efficient inference)
  4. Verify correctness: For each completion, run the verifier:
    • Math: Parse final answer (regex or LLM-based extraction), compare to ground truth
    • IFEval: Check all constraints (e.g., word count, formatting, keyword inclusion)
  5. RL optimization: Use PPO to maximize the expected reward (probability of generating correct completions)

Infrastructure: Asynchronous RL at Scale

Training 70B and 405B models with RL requires efficient infrastructure. Tülu 3's RLVR setup:

  • Inference: Run on VLLM (optimized for throughput) to generate completions quickly
  • Training: Learner nodes perform gradient updates concurrently while inference runs
  • Asynchronous pipeline: Inference and training don't block each other, maximizing GPU utilization

This architecture allowed Tülu 3 to train 405B RLVR models feasibly.

RLVR Results

From Table 5 (70B model):

  • GSM8K: 91.1% (SFT) → 93.5% (DPO) → 93.5% (RLVR, maintained)
  • MATH: 53.7% (SFT) → 62.3% (DPO) → 63.0% (RLVR, +0.7 points)
  • IFEval: 82.1% (SFT) → 82.6% (DPO) → 83.2% (RLVR, +0.6 points)
  • Safety: 94.4% (SFT) → 89.0% (DPO) → 88.3% (RLVR, slight further drop)

⚠️ Safety Regression in RLVR

The safety score dropped from 94.4% (SFT) to 88.3% (RLVR). This is expected: RLVR optimizes for math and IF performance, not safety. The paper notes this tradeoff and suggests future work could include safety-specific RLVR rewards or a final safety finetuning stage.

Tülu 3 Eval: Development vs Unseen Benchmarks

A critical innovation in Tülu 3 is its rigorous evaluation framework with separate development and unseen (held-out) benchmark splits. This prevents overfitting to specific evaluation sets during training.

Development Benchmarks: Guide Training

Development benchmarks are used throughout training to measure progress and inform decisions about data mixtures, hyperparameters, and training duration. For Tülu 3, development benchmarks include:

Knowledge

MMLU (0-shot, CoT): Massive multitask language understanding

PopQA (15-shot): Entity-centric question answering

TruthfulQA (6-shot): Truthful answers to adversarial questions

Reasoning

BigBenchHard (3-shot, CoT): Challenging BigBench subset

DROP (3-shot): Discrete reasoning over paragraphs

Math

MATH (4-shot, CoT, Flex): Competition-level math problems

GSM8K (8-shot, CoT): Grade-school math word problems

Coding

HumanEval (pass@10): Python function synthesis

HumanEval+ (pass@10): HumanEval with extra test cases

Instruction Following & Chat

IFEval (prompt loose): Verifiable instruction following

AlpacaEval 2 (LC % win): Conversational quality vs reference

Safety

Tülu 3 Safety: Average across 6 safety tasks (details in paper Section 7.2.1)

Unseen Benchmarks: Detect Overfitting

Unseen benchmarks are held out during training—the team did not look at scores on these until final evaluation. This reveals how much the training process overfit to development benchmarks. Unseen benchmarks include:

  • Knowledge: MMLU-Pro, GPQA
  • Reasoning: AGIEval English
  • Math: Deepmind Mathematics
  • Coding: BigCodeBench
  • Instruction Following: IFEval-OOD, HREF

Why Separate Splits Matter

When you iterate on training data and hyperparameters while constantly checking the same benchmarks, you inevitably overfit—not to individual examples (that's contamination), but to the distribution and style of the benchmark. Unseen benchmarks provide an unbiased estimate of generalization.

Tülu 3 finding: Performance on development and unseen benchmarks was highly correlated, suggesting the training process generalized well and didn't severely overfit to development evals.

Decontamination: No Evaluation Leakage

Tülu 3 systematically decontaminated all training data against all evaluation benchmarks:

  1. Exact match: Remove training examples that exactly match any evaluation example
  2. Fuzzy match: Use text similarity (n-gram overlap, embedding cosine similarity) to catch near-duplicates
  3. Manual review: Inspect flagged examples to confirm contamination
  4. Report statistics: Document how much contamination was found in each dataset (see Table 37 in the paper)

⚠️ Contamination is Widespread

Tülu 3 found non-trivial contamination in several popular open datasets. Many publicly released datasets were scraped from the web or synthesized by LLMs that had access to benchmark data, leading to accidental leakage. Decontamination is not optional—it's essential for honest evaluation.

Bridge to the Curriculum

How Tülu 3 Unifies Earlier Lessons

Lesson 01: MMLU & Lesson 09: HELM

Tülu 3 Eval builds on HELM's holistic evaluation philosophy: measure multiple skills, use development/unseen splits, and decontaminate training data. Tülu 3 shows how to integrate rigorous benchmarking into the training loop—not just for final evaluation.

Lesson 02: InstructGPT & RLHF

Tülu 3's SFT stage follows InstructGPT's SFT approach but with more diverse, skill-targeted data. RLVR is a variant of RL from Lesson 02, but uses verifiable rewards instead of a learned reward model, avoiding reward hacking.

Lesson 04: DPO

Tülu 3 uses length-normalized DPO (Stage 3) to refine the SFT model via on-policy preference data. This demonstrates DPO at scale with careful data curation—extending the DPO lesson with real-world best practices.

Lesson 06: GRPO & Verifiable Rewards

RLVR shares the core idea with GRPO: use verifiable rewards (unit tests, math verification) instead of learned reward models. Tülu 3 extends this to instruction following (IFEval) and scales to 405B models.

Lesson 08: Constitutional AI

Constitutional AI uses AI-generated preference labels to reduce human annotation. Tülu 3's on-policy DPO data generation is similar: use strong models (or human judges) to label preferences for the current model's outputs. Both scale supervision efficiently.

Lesson 03: GSM8K & Lesson 07: HumanEval

Tülu 3 targets these specific benchmarks with synthetic data (Persona GSM, Persona Python) and RLVR optimization. This shows how to translate benchmark-specific insights (from Lessons 03 and 07) into training data and objectives that improve those benchmarks.

The Complete Post-Training Picture

Across Lessons 02, 04, 06, 08, and now 10, you've learned individual post-training techniques. Tülu 3 shows how to combine them into a coherent pipeline:

  1. Start with diverse, decontaminated data (synthetic + real-world prompts)
  2. SFT to establish foundations (mix general + skill-specific data)
  3. DPO to refine chat quality (on-policy preferences, length normalization)
  4. RLVR to push verifiable tasks (math, IF, code—use ground truth, not learned RM)
  5. Evaluate rigorously (dev/unseen splits, decontamination, report tradeoffs)

And critically: release everything. Tülu 3 demonstrates that open post-training can achieve state-of-the-art results when done carefully—no proprietary data or secret sauce required.
